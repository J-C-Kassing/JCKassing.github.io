**Authors**: J.-C. Kassing, F. Frohn, and J. Giesl

There are many evaluation strategies for term rewrite systems, but proving termination automatically is usually easiest for innermost rewriting. Several syntactic criteria exist when innermost termination implies full termination. We adapt these criteria to the probabilistic setting, e.g., we show when it suffices to analyze almost-sure termination (AST) w.r.t. innermost rewriting to prove full AST of probabilistic term rewrite systems. These criteria also apply to other notions of termination like positive AST. We implemented and evaluated our new contributions in the tool AProVE.

ERRATUM
=======

Theorem 27 and Theorem 38 are incorrect
---------------------------------------

Theorem 27 claims that for every non-overlapping and right-linear PTRS S, iAST (iPAST)
w.r.t. innermost simultaneous rewriting implies fAST (fPAST). Theorem 38 claims the
same on basic start terms for every non-overlapping and spare PTRS. Both theorems are
false. The same holds for their PAST/SAST versions in App. B of the extended version.

### Counterexample

Let s = h(f(a,a)) and let P consist of the rules

  h(x)       -> {1/2: h1(x), 1/2: h2(x)}
  a          -> {1/2: b, 1/2: c}
  f(x,x)     -> {1: x}
  h1(b)      -> {1: s}
  h1(c)      -> {1: s}
  h2(f(b,c)) -> {1: d(s,s,s)}
  h2(f(c,b)) -> {1: e(s,s)}

P is non-overlapping and right-linear, hence also spare.

- P is not fAST, not even on the basic start term h1(b) (which rewrites to s).
  Under full rewriting, a rewrite strategy can proceed as follows: on h1(f(a,a)), first
  apply the f-rule; on h2(f(a,a)), first rewrite both a's separately. Then every copy of
  s produces 0, 1, 2, or 3 new copies of s with probabilities 1/4, 1/2, 1/8, and 1/8.
  This is a branching process with mean 9/8 > 1, so it terminates only with probability
  sqrt(3) - 1 < 1.
- P is iAST, and even iSAST, w.r.t. innermost simultaneous rewriting. In innermost
  evaluation, the two a's in f(a,a) have to be rewritten before the f-rule can be
  applied. If they are rewritten simultaneously, s produces 1/2 new copies of s in
  expectation; if they are rewritten one after another, it produces 7/8. In both cases,
  the process is subcritical.

So P refutes Theorem 27 and Theorem 38 for AST, PAST, and SAST. The proofs only work
for terms with at most two nested defined symbols.

Consequences
------------
- The claims in Sect. 1 and Sect. 5 that left-linearity can be dropped by using
  simultaneous rewriting are withdrawn. Def. 24, Cor. 25, Cor. 29, and
  Counterexamples 26, 28, and 30 are correct, but they are only relevant for the
  withdrawn theorems.
- Sect. 6 (evaluation): the results that rely on Theorem 27 or Theorem 38 are not
  supported. These are
  - the columns "Thm. 27" (44) and "Thm. 38" (56) of the table and the corresponding
    part of the diagram;
  - the 3 examples that "can only be solved by Thm. 38", so for basic start terms,
    61 drops to 58;
  - the increase from 48 to 49 examples for arbitrary start terms, so 49 drops to 48.

- The strategy of AProVE described in Sect. 6 is unsound for PTRSs that are not
  left-linear.
- All other results are not affected. This includes Theorems 17, 20, 22, and 33 and
  Lemma 35.
